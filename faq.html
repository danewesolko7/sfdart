About SF-DART

What is SF-DART?

SF-DART is a tool from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). The name stands for Single Family Data Analysis and Reporting Tool.

Servicers use it to see their Tier Ranking System II (TRS II) scores. They can also see the loans behind each score and read quarterly reports.

Who uses SF-DART?

HUD staff and servicers approved by the Federal Housing Administration (FHA). Only people with an account can sign in.

What data is in SF-DART?

  • Seven years of FHA mortgage data.
  • Details on loans in default.
  • Mortgage insurance claims from servicers.
  • Home Equity Conversion Mortgage data.

Reports cover servicing, defaults, claims and how well loss mitigation works. You can group them by area, HUD office, lender or loss mitigation program.

Accounts and sign-in

How do I get an account?

Your organization's staff sign up for an SF-DART account. For help getting access, email sfdart-support@hud.gov.

How do I sign in?

Go to the sign-in page. Enter your username and select Agree and continue. You'll enter your password on the next screen.

What am I agreeing to when I select "Agree and continue"?

You accept HUD's legal notice and the Security and Usage Agreement (PDF). Signing in counts as your signature on that agreement.

I can't sign in. What should I do?

Email the SF-DART Help Desk at sfdart-support@hud.gov. They help with sign-in, account access and errors in reports.

TRS II scores and tiers

What does TRS II measure?

TRS II grades servicers every quarter. It looks at four parts of how they handle loans in default:

  • Foreclosure Prevention: how often the servicer tried loss mitigation before starting a foreclosure.
  • Redefaults: how loans do in the six months after a modification or partial claim.
  • SFDMS Reporting: how well the servicer follows the rules for reporting to SFDMS. This includes fatal errors and neglected defaults.
  • Loss Mitigation Engagement: how many loss mitigation claims succeeded, and how often the servicer reported working with borrowers.

How are scores worked out?

Scores are worked out every month. The months are averaged into a quarterly score. The quarters are then averaged into a yearly score. The year runs from October 1 to September 30.

How do grades map to tiers?

Grades, tiers and yearly score ranges
Grade Tier Yearly score
ATier 190–100%
BTier 280–89.99%
C or DTier 360–79.99%
FTier 4Below 60%

Tier 1 is the best. Tier 1 servicers get servicing incentives.

How will I know when new scores are posted?

You get an email when new quarterly scores are posted.

Can I see the loans behind my score?

Yes. Each part of your score links to the FHA cases behind it, so you can see how each loan performed.

Where can I read how TRS II works in full?

Read the TRS II servicer narrative (PDF). For policy questions, call the FHA Resource Center at (800) 225-5342.

Terms used on this site

Case
One FHA-insured loan. Each has its own FHA case number.
Default
When a borrower is behind on their mortgage payments.
FHA
Federal Housing Administration. The part of HUD that insures home loans.
Foreclosure
The legal process a lender uses to take back a home when the borrower can't pay.
Home Equity Conversion Mortgage
FHA's reverse mortgage for older homeowners.
Loss mitigation
Ways a servicer helps a borrower who is behind on payments avoid foreclosure.
Modification
A change to the terms of a loan so the borrower can afford the payments.
Mortgage insurance claim
A request a servicer sends FHA to be paid for a loss on an insured loan.
Partial claim
Money HUD lends a borrower, with no interest, to catch up on missed payments. It is paid back when the loan is paid off or the home is sold.
Redefault
When a borrower falls behind again after a modification or partial claim.
Servicer
A company that collects mortgage payments and works with borrowers who fall behind.
SFDMS
Single Family Default Monitoring System. The HUD system servicers use to report loans in default.
Tier
A servicer's overall TRS II ranking, from Tier 1 (best) to Tier 4 (lowest).
TRS II
Tier Ranking System II. How HUD grades servicers each quarter.
